Day skydome (August 2026): the flat tan card is gone — Kenney's Skyboxes pack day panorama (`public/sky/skybox-day.png`, 4096×2048 equirect, CC0) on an inside-out `CreateSphere` in `sceneSetup.ts` (BACKSIDE + `infiniteDistance`, emissive-only StandardMaterial, `applyFog = false`; standard sphere UVs *are* equirectangular, so no cubemap face-splitting) with `clearColor`/`fogColor` retuned from `#e9c98f` tan to `#b3c6f1` — the panorama's just-above-horizon average — so the fog band dissolves geometry into sky. Sett streets (August 2026): paved roads left `drawPaving`'s uniform running bond for `drawSetts` (paths.ts) — five courses of random-width, jitter-cornered setts over darker grout, so streets read medieval against the plazas'/aprons' finished slabs (which keep `drawPaving`). Zoomed-out tile repetition is broken by 4 seed variants per material hashed by cell position (`texVariant`/one thin-instance batch per variant in roadRenderer.ts). Cross-variant edge contract in the drawer: even courses end on a grout joint exactly at the texture edge, odd courses share one deterministic edge-crossing stone seeded independently of the variant — drop either half and tile boundaries cut stones mid-course or the edge joints align into a visible full-height seam. Junction pads accumulate across the ribbon variant batches (each holds only its hash slice; `flush` sets the pad buffers once after all ribbon batches flush — a per-batch set would clobber the others' pads). The default plaza style (`drawPlazaCobble` ring cobbles) followed in the same pass — sett grout, jittered stones — so the plaza reads as kin to the streets; the herringbone/travertine alternates and the plaza border slabs still use `drawPaving`'s finished bond.

Rialto hump (August 2026): bridge decks ascend/descend over a run — `bridgeProfile.ts` computes a parabolic lift per contiguous bridge run (zero at the outer edges so decks meet roads flush; `rise = min(0.16, 0.05·n)`), and every deck-riding piece in `flushBridges` (deck quad, long-side rails, arch masonry) takes one chord transform per cell: yaw maps local +X onto the travel direction, roll climbs the chord, x-stretch keeps the horizontal footprint at one cell, so adjacent chords form a continuous low-poly arch. Masonry additionally y-stretches (base pinned at −0.38) so arches grow taller toward the crown. Runs group by `bridgeStep` (diagonals by rotation, cardinals by the rails' open-axis rule) and lane-mates hump in lockstep since both lanes share the span. Walkers sample the same `bridgeLiftAt` (citizens.ts adds it to FOOT_Y) — renderer and figures can't drift apart. Known nits: land-placed bridges hump too (reads as a raised causeway), and diagonal chord joints show hairline seams at extreme zoom.

Stone arch bridge (August 2026): bridges grew under-deck masonry (`arches` in roadRenderer.ts) — per cell, two piers dropping to below the river bed with a segmental arch between them (crown just under the deck, springline underwater — spring −0.145/r 0.15, tuned to the 0.105 wu between deck 0.025 and water −0.08), thin-instanced on the parapet material so a run reads as a stone arcade. Faces sit at ±0.24, flush with the parapets' outer face; cardinal orientation derives from the rails' openAt rule (travel = open axis, x wins ties), diagonals ride the deck's yaw + √2 x-stretch. Quad winding is resolved per-face against the outward normal (Babylon LH: front face = RH cross opposite the normal) so no face can come out inverted.

Cathedral transept + wide body (August 2026): the basilica bar became Santa Croce's T over four review rounds — final shape: a 3-unit nave (walls z ±1.5) with 1.5-unit aisles (blocks z-scaled 1.5 at ±2.25, walls ±3 — the one deliberate non-unit wall; its 1.5× course stretch shows only on the small front slabs and was accepted), crossed by a uniform 2.0-tall transept crossbar of seven columns spanning z ±3.5, fitted out to the footprint edge (`fit` 0.98, "full width of the tiles"), 0.7 units deep at x −1.7..−1 (front flush with the aisle ends so the lean-tos bury into it; the 0.7× course squeeze on its rubble end faces is illegible). The nave runs the FULL length x −2..2 — a fourth column row passes it clean through the crossbar, its back wall projecting 0.3 behind as a chancel stub under a small rear pediment. The cross roof pitches like an ordinary house (0.7 y-scale — HIGH_GABLE spiked on the shallow crossbar), ridge 2.4 under the nave's eave line so the arms die into the nave's side walls. Lesson from rounds 2–4: a nave that stops short of its roof's rear run leaves a visible hollow between end wall and roof underside — walls must underlie the full roof; shifting the crossbar around (rearward round 2, forward round 3) never fixed that, filling the nave through did. CATH_BAYS respaced to the clear wall run (−0.6..1.8): bays behind the transept's front wall bury in the crossbar or poke through its lower roof. Fit gotcha: the z-extent growth flipped stretch-fit's min axis from x to z and shrank it, so `scaleY` rose 0.71 → 1.28 (`CATH_SQUASH`) to keep the built height — and the west rose's pre-stretch is that reciprocal, now exactly round since min *is* scaleZ. Two knock-ons fixed in the same pass: front fittings rendered 1.28× squished (world y stretches, facade horizontal doesn't) — `unsquish` widens each posX-face part's local z by CATH_SQUASH to restore authored proportions (portals + bifore; heights are absolute wall coords and hold; other faces carry different stretches, don't reuse it there) — and the nave roof's old 0.15 front verge left its ridge cap hanging past the facade in open air ("floating roof"), so the roof now ends flush at the slab planes (±2.05). The rose grew to 1.65 @ yCenter 1.905 — the ceiling for the band: the ring (radius 0.35×s) must stay between the portal top (1.30) and the slab top (2.5), above which the recessed pediment leaves it floating in open air. Two nave-front bifore at z ±1 (same yOpen/scale as the aisle rows) complete the west-front rhythm.

Big-roof scale fixes (August 2026), both surfaced by the transept's 3× nave roof: (1) the per-row tile lap (`LAP` in proceduralPieces.ts) accumulated linearly up the slope — 12 rows × the 3× z-stretch floated the top rows and ridge cap ~0.15wu off the core — so `lapStep(rows)` now normalizes the per-row lift to keep the total stack at the house height on any row count (houses, rows=4, are bit-identical; check pins a 12-row roof's ridge to the house roof's). (2) `proc:gable-end`'s planar UVs bake the house assumption (unit walls, 0.6 roof squash), so the light-brick pediment rendered ~3×4 giant courses against the facade — textured gables at other scales now pass UV corrections in the id (`proc:gable-end@<u>x<v>` via the manifest's `gableEndFile(scale, courseH)`, `courseH` = the wall-below's world height per texture wrap; `gableRoof` takes it as an opt). Bare-id gable-ends are untouched.

Raze feedback (August 2026): the raze tool shells the hovered building in the ghost-invalid red via `instantiateTransientModel` (mapRenderer — the batcher's exact clone, seated like `createEntry`; scaled 1.02 so the shell can't z-fight the batched mesh; roads keep the flat quad highlight), and committing a raze fires a building-volume dust burst (`razeFx.ts`, real-time, cosmetic-only; `spawnRazeFx` is a module-level handler slot so the DOM RazeConfirm can fire it — always call it BEFORE `removeTile`, it reads the still-standing tile from the store). A sink-out clone rode the first cut and was reverted — the building sliding into the ground read comical; the dust carries the whole effect, so it fills the footprint volume (0.6 tall emit box, sizes 0.35–0.6 growing 2.2×), not just a ground skirt. Two traps: `disposeOnStop` queues a full `dispose()` whose default `disposeTexture=true` destroys the SHARED puff texture (smoke.ts) — first raze worked, every later burst and the chimney smoke rendered nothing; cleanup is `onStoppedObservable` → `dispose(false)` instead. And hover targeting can't be the ground-plane pick alone (pointing at a wall/roof lands on the cell BEHIND the building) — `pickBuildingBody` in placement.ts ray-tests footprint-AABBs (design height, nearest box wins) and feeds tooltip, highlight, and click alike; the ground cell stays as the road fallback.

Door person-scaling (August 2026): ordinary doors normalized toward human proportion (person ≈ 0.83× door) off a live world-height audit — workshop/architect 0.83wu, sculptor/marble 0.79, quarry 0.77, pigment/palazzo 0.75, bell-tower portal 0.85 (was 0.67, smaller than a cottage door); residential/tavern/warehouse (0.84–0.87) were already right. The ceiling rule: `proc:door-frame` is 0.81 units tall (0.75 opening + lintel), so a door's y-scale must keep 0.81×scale under the wall-below's unit height — most non-residential storeys render ~0.77wu, capping those doors at ~0.75–0.83wu world. The bakery is the worked example: its 0.75-unit shop bay caps the door at 0.60wu (y 0.92); a person-proportioned door there needs a taller bay.

Grass tone field + finer facets (August 2026): meadows stop being one even green — a seeded two-sine-octave field (`makeMeadowField` in terrain.ts, same idiom/null-seed contract as `makeHillHeight`) blends facets toward scorched `STRAW_TONES` at its peaks and `DUSTY_TONE` at its troughs, and water distance blends toward `LUSH_TONES` along banks and coast — still exactly one tone per facet (the field only moves *which* tone a facet lands on; intra-facet mottle was tested and rejected — at visible strength it's unstructured smudge, screenshots in the session notes). Override order in the color loop matters: base grass → tone field → farmland patches → shore/bed, so fields stay farmland and banks stay sand. SUBDIVISIONS rose 160 → 240 (1.33-wu facets) in the same pass — banks and hill shoulders sculpt finer; 320 was tested and rejected (per-facet tone hashing reads as noisy checkerboard on open meadow, and facet-normal contrast washes out under the gentle-slope guarantee). Everything downstream (`surfaceAt`, groundLevel, bank rims) derives from SUBDIVISIONS and followed automatically.

Paved verge (August 2026): hard paving stopped cutting a razor vector edge into the grass — the dirt overlay (`dirtPathOverlay.ts`) gained a pass 0 that halos every paved surface with worn earth, reusing the chunking/draping/redraw-budget machinery rather than growing a second sheet. Per cell it fills the rect inflated only on sides where `open()` says the neighbor is unoccupied (so abutting roads, aprons, and buildings stay flush and only city-to-grass edges get a fringe), drawn as one union path in `VERGE_RINGS` passes of shrinking inflation so alpha stacks toward the paved edge and feathers out; `vergeW`/`VERGE_TONE` are the taste knobs. The trap: the ring fills MUST be erased back to the plain cell rects (`destination-out`) before the dirt passes — streets sit at y 0.01 above the 0.008 sheet and would hide an unerased interior, but aprons and plaza pads sit at 0.005 BELOW it, so the fill would smear ~0.55 alpha of earth across all their flagstone. Feed cells come from `mapRenderer`'s `pavedCells`: paved-road tiles (dirt and bridges excluded — bridges hang over water) plus every claimed cell of a `metadata.paved` building, which is exactly what its apron/plaza pad covers since both are fit-scaled to the footprint. Diagonal buildings contribute their diamond mask cells (the tile map keys each claimed cell separately, so the bounding-box trap doesn't apply), which leaves the 45° apron's spilled corners slightly outside the halo — invisible at the fringe's softness.

Roof mosaic variants (August 2026): every instance of one roof piece id shared one baked per-tile shade mosaic (`hashShade` keyed only on slope/course/row), so same-size roofs twinned tile-for-tile across the city. Roof piece ids now take a `~salt` suffix (`proc:roof-gable@51x7~2`, `ROOF_MOSAIC_VARIANTS` = 3; bare id is variant 0) parsed by `buildProceduralContainer` and threaded into every `hashShade` call; `instantiateBuilding` picks the salt by position hash (`hash >> 9`). Two integration traps: (1) `instantiatePart` resolves containers synchronously from the preload cache, so `partFiles()` expands a roof part to all its variant twins for both `addModelFiles` *and* the `hasModel` gate — miss either and buildings instantiate wall-complete but roofless; (2) the batching `buildHosts` parsed mesh keys (`file#i~tint`) with `indexOf("~")`, which the salt's own `~` broke silently (`containers.get` missed → mesh skipped) — the tint separator must be searched after the `#`. Within one large roof, `hashShade` needs its fmix32 finalizer: FNV alone leaves the low bits weakly mixed for small sequential course/row keys and `% 8` reads only those bits, which rendered the cathedral aisles as a periodic diagonal lattice instead of noise.

Gathering overlay (August 2026): the first map-overlay view (`gatheringOverlay.ts`) — one draped shared-vertex grid mesh (121² verts, corner heights from `worldGroundY` + 0.035 lift, built lazily on first show like the placement grid, since the ground sampler registers late) carrying a 480² DynamicTexture painted per cell (heat wash + formed-plaza fills/borders), on the sky's unlit recipe (emissive+diffuse same texture, `useAlphaFromDiffuseTexture`, `disableLighting`, NEAREST sampling; UV v = 1 − j/G so canvas row j is grid row j). Redraws only when tiles identity or seed changes while visible. Babylon trap found while shipping it: geometry-less container meshes (`new Mesh` holders, GLB `__root__`s) leave `subMeshes` **undefined**, and the scene ready-check (`scene.pure.js`, checkRenderTargets path) iterates `mesh.subMeshes` unguarded for any mesh falling back to the default material — per-frame console errors whenever a ready-loop is pending (model streaming, a new material compiling; the overlay's fresh material surfaced it, but main had transient load-time hits too). Weathering + sky-life pass (August 2026): three quiet realism layers. (1) Rising-damp footing — every `proc:block` merges a DAMP_H-tall footing box into the wall so `shadeByPosition` gets a vertex row there, dipping the base an extra DAMP below the AO ramp (a box interpolates corner-to-corner and can't hold the curve); baked into the shared mesh, so elevated refs carry a faint band, accepted as floor-line shadow — and the check's block color pin moved to the new vertex-mean (the footing overweights it; area-weighted the wall is only ~2% darker). (2) Cypress avenues — short formal rows (4–7 trees, one variant + one base scale per row) join the random cypress/olive scatter in environmentScatter.ts. (3) Cloud shadows — `cloudShadows.ts`, a global material plugin multiplying lit color by a scrolling soft blob texture sampled at `vPositionW.xz` before fog (no second light, no per-frame shadow map, thin-instance-safe; sky excluded by material name; real-time like the sky, frozen under reduced motion; CLOUD_SPAN/WIND/CORE_TINT the knobs). Two plugin traps: the `getUniforms` key is `ubo` — the older documented `ub` slips past TypeScript's weak-type check, the uniform never lands in the UBO, and EVERY StandardMaterial fails to compile (world renders empty); and Babylon unregisters ALL material plugins whenever an engine disposes (menu round-trips, React's dev double-mount), so `RegisterMaterialPlugin` must run on every scene create, not behind a once-flag — it dedupes by name.

Shutters (August 2026): `proc:louvres` — a louvred shutter pair over every house window (`houseWindow` = `windowOn` + `louvresOn` in the manifest; the workshops/stores that also call `windowOn` stay bare), green `wood` leaves of back slab + stiles/rails + 7 recessed slats, lapping `LOUVRE_LAP` onto the surround all round. Three things carry the pass: (1) **open and closed are one piece**, `proc:louvres` / `proc:louvres~1`, riding the roof mosaics' existing `~salt` path — so the manifest holds one part at one position and `instantiateBuilding` picks the state, each variant its own thin-instance host (`partFiles` must list the twin or the open host never loads and `hasModel` gates on a file that isn't there). (2) The open leaves **swing 142° out**, laid back toward the facade but never flat on it, and that is the physical ceiling rather than a taste call: sideways reach is `w(1 − cos swing) + t·sin swing` and two facing leaves must not cross between windows a townhouse column pitch (0.25) apart. A leaf is half a window wide, so folding flat wants a full window-width of bare wall beside each opening and the townhouse leaves 0.12 — every degree flatter is bought sideways, and `LOUVRE_LAP` is trimmed to 0.002 to buy the last few. Leaves jut 0.046 off the wall, ~0.003 of slack left between facing tips. Their hinge plane stays at local x = 0 in *both* states — that is what lets one position serve either — which makes the open twin the one piece deliberately not x-centered, so it is checked explicitly rather than by the blanket loop. Parts are `buried`: an open shutter overhanging its cell is the point, and the footprint fit must not shrink the house to swallow it (side windows on an abutting terrace bury in the neighbour's wall, as their surrounds already did). (3) Which window gets which state is **the one thing in the city that isn't position-hashed** — `SHUTTER_SEED` in `assetLibrary.ts` is a fresh `Math.random()` per page load, mixed with the cell (so one house's windows don't all match) and the part slot (so its windows don't match each other), so the same save wakes up differently every session. Render-only: no sim reads it, no save carries it, and screenshots of the same city will not reproduce shutter-for-shutter.
